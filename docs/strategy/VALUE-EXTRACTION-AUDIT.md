## 2026-09-02 — re-measured against the 500-cartridge commons

> _Note (2026-09-04):_ the generated table above/below is regenerated on every commons
> change and now reads **495**; the prose in this section still says 500, and is left as
> written. Five cartridges (`keyv2`, `stemfie`, `multiboard`, `polydice`, `rugged-box`)
> were removed whole under ADR-021 with their slugs reserved for clean-room re-creation,
> and `gridfinity` lost its OpenSCAD side, taking dual-engine from 21 to 19. Where this
> section's prose and the generated table disagree, the table is current.

**Why.** The 2026-08 audit reasoned in ratios over 326, and 326 is gone. Every "x of
326" below was a claim about a repo that no longer exists, so the findings had drifted
from decorative to misleading — a reader could not tell which gaps had closed, which had
widened, and which had simply been diluted by growth.

**Method.** `scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py` recomputes every ratio the 2026-08
section states, from `projects/*/project.json`, `docs/commons-catalog.json`, the studio
locale files, and the CDG family taxonomy in `apps/api`. The table below is generated:
`--write` refreshes it, `--check` fails CI when the committed numbers drift from a fresh
recomputation, and `--json` emits the same data machine-readably. Two guards make the
numbers trustworthy rather than merely printable. First, the on-disk cartridge count is
asserted against `counts.cartridges` in the committed catalog before anything is
measured. Since RFC 0038 P2 the whole commons is ONE submodule at `projects/`
(`madfam-org/solid-hyperobjects`), so an uninitialised checkout loses **all** 500
cartridges rather than a subset, and the table would still print. Before P2 the
exposure was partial and worse for being partial: 34 of the 500 cartridges were
separate satellite repos and every dual-engine cartridge was among them. (Two
earlier statements of that figure disagreed — this doc said 34 and the script's
docstring said 35; 34 was right. `.gitmodules` declared 37 paths under
`projects/`, of which two were the client-private `tablaco*` entries and one the
`cq-hyperobject-test` fixture the catalog excludes.) Second, the cartridges the Commons catalog does not publish are
excluded by importing the generator's own exclusion set rather than restating it, so this
audit and `COMMONS.md` can never describe different commons.

**The baseline reproduces.** `--cohorts` splits today's commons into the **324** published
cartridges that already existed at the audit commit `f2cff0d7` and the **176** that
arrived after it. Recomputing the 2026-08 rows over just the first cohort returns the
audit's published figures exactly: 300 `engine: cadquery`, 287 cartridges with
constraints, 310 with `material_awareness`, and 294 / 30 / 16 for the three material
capability flags. Camera-view and preset coverage land one cartridge low (292 and 302,
against the published 293 and 303) because the 326 denominator counted every directory
under `projects/`, including ones the catalog excludes. That single cartridge is the
entire discrepancy — the extraction logic used here is the logic that produced the
original numbers, which is what makes the delta column meaningful rather than a
comparison of two different measurements.

### What moved, and why

**The fourth and fifth hundreds.** The 176 cartridges added since the audit are not the
cohort `docs/strategy/FOURTH-100-STRATEGY.md` planned. That plan asked the fourth hundred
to skew toward the thin domains, and on its own terms it delivered: medical 29 → 44,
infrastructure 20 → 33, hybrid 7 → 14, soft-robotics 1 → 7, with agriculture (5), consumer
(2), construction (1) and energy (1) appearing as domains for the first time. But the
single largest block in the 176 is `wearable` — 72 cartridges, 41% of everything added,
and zero of them existed when the audit ran. In composition the fifth hundred is the
Fashion Cabinet supply campaign of `docs/strategy/WEARABLES-COVERAGE.md`, not a
graph-filling exercise. The quality signature differs accordingly: the new cohort is
denser on interfaces (2.27 CDG interfaces per cartridge against the audit-era cohort's
1.68) and thinner on curated depth (1.88 camera views against 2.71, 2.76 presets against
3.15, 1.16 constraints against 1.71). Growth raised most coverage percentages while
diluting per-object metadata — a distinction the 326-era ratios could not have shown.

**The FC bridge.** The supply side of the cross-commons contract is now measurable from
this repo: 72 cartridges filed `domain: wearable`, 49 exposing a `flange` interface (the
dimensional handshake FC consumes), and 53 whose README carries the "Fashion Cabinet
bridge" section that names the garments expected to use them. All 49 flange cartridges and
all 53 bridge READMEs are wearables, and all of them arrived after the audit — at
`f2cff0d7` every one of these three counts was zero. The three numbers are deliberately
unequal: `WEARABLES-COVERAGE` principle 3 says flange only where fabric mates, so
point-fixed hardware declares socket/snap/custom instead. What the gap between them does
expose is a documentation debt against principle 4, which asks *every* cartridge on the
shelf to end its README with the bridge section: 19 of the 72 wearables have no such
section, and 18 cartridges declare a flange interface without one. The number this repo
cannot produce is how many FC links actually resolve — that lives on the Fashion Cabinet
side of the contract, and inventing a proxy for it here would be exactly the kind of
number this re-measurement exists to remove.

**Dual-engine cartridges.** 21 of 500. Every one predates the audit and came from
what was then a standalone satellite repo (absorbed into the commons by RFC 0038 P2), and not one of the 176 new cartridges ships an OpenSCAD mode
alongside its CadQuery one — so the number only ever moves downward. It stood at 22 until
#115 found that `implicit-lattice-hyperobject` declared an OpenSCAD kernel it never
implemented and dropped the claim. As a share it is 4.2%, down from 6.8% of the audit-era
cohort — dilution, plus that one correction. Read together with 474 of 500 declaring
`engine: cadquery` and 490 offering STEP export, the commons has
settled on one kernel; all ten OpenSCAD-only cartridges are inherited from former
standalone repos. This row was the one most exposed to the submodule-empty trap — with the
satellite submodules uninitialised it read 0 while every other row still looked
reasonable, which is why the script refuses to print anything until the on-disk count
matches the catalog. Under P2 that failure is no longer selective: an uninitialised
`projects` submodule zeroes every row at once, and `validate_manifests.py`'s ratchet
refuses the checkout outright.

**Quadrilingual coverage.** de/fr/pt/zh carry 1,040 of 1,480 strings, 70.3% — down 5.6 pp
from the audit's ~75.8%. Nothing was un-translated. The studio's key set grew from 327 to
370, the four locales absorbed the new keys as `[UNTRANSLATED]` markers, and the per-locale
marker count went from ~79 to 110. Key parity is intact (six files, 370 keys each), which
is precisely why a parity gate cannot see this regression, and why the mechanism audit #6
described is unchanged: `resolveTranslation` in
`apps/studio/src/contexts/system/LanguageProvider.tsx:26` returns the stored string
whenever it is truthy, and `"[UNTRANSLATED] …"` is truthy, so the marker reaches the user
instead of the English fallback. **The CI half of #6 has since closed**: `#86` gave
`scripts/qa/i18n_audit.py` its own blocking `i18n-audit` job, reached by `ci-success`, with
key parity as a hard gate and the hardcoded-string count ratcheted against
`scripts/qa/i18n_baseline.json`. The parity gate is exactly the gate that cannot see this
regression, so every key added still widens the gap by construction until either the
translations land or the fallback stops treating the marker as a translation.

**Constraints.** The one metric that fell hard: 400 of 500 cartridges carry constraints
(80.0%, −8.0 pp) at 1.52 per cartridge, down from 1.70. The cohort split shows this is
authoring drift, not erosion — 88.6% of the audit-era cohort declares constraints against
64.2% of the 176 added since; no existing cartridge lost any. Audit #11 shipped the repair
for the constraints that existed (the evaluator read `constraint.rule` while all 555
authored constraints used `expression`, so live validation was silently dead), and the
waves authored after that repair shipped constraints less often. The capability was fixed
and the data feeding it thinned at the same time, which is the shape of regression a
one-off audit is structurally unable to catch.

**Camera views and presets.** Coverage rose on both — 91.2% and 94.0% of cartridges — while
density fell to 2.42 angles and 3.01 presets each. In absolute volume the commons now holds
1,210 curated angles (+37%) and 1,505 proven configurations (+47%). The 2026-08 REFUTED
finding still stands: those angles are not dead, they drive the in-viewer guided-view
switcher. So do the two opportunities attached to them — #10 (angles → auto-thumbnails) and
#7 (a commons-wide preset index) — and both grew roughly 40% more valuable on volume alone
without any new capability being needed.

**Material awareness.** 484 of 500 cartridges (96.8%) carry the block. The movement worth
reading is inside it: `recycled_material_toggle` went 16 → 98 and `shrinkage_compensation`
30 → 68. `FOURTH-100-STRATEGY` principle 4 singled out exactly those two flags as rare and
therefore facet-valuable, and the cohort split shows the instruction landed — 46.6% of new
cartridges declare `recycled_material_toggle` against 4.9% of the audit-era set. This is
the clearest case in the table of a strategy document measurably changing what got
authored.

**CDG families and the standards moat.** 486 of 500 cartridges declare an interface (942 in
total, 1.88 each), and 210 resolve to a named standard family, up from ~98. That 42% is two
changes at once and should not be read as one: the taxonomy itself grew from 61 keys to
116, and re-running today's taxonomy over the audit-era cohort alone resolves 149 of its
324 — so most of the jump is a wider net, not newer objects. The 176 new cartridges resolve
at 34.7%, *below* the audit-era cohort's 46.0%. 113 of the 116 family keys now have at
least one member. The 276 cartridges that declare an interface but resolve to no family are
the honest measure of how much headroom #5 and #9 still have.

**The public discovery surface.** **All 500 published cartridges are now reachable from the
landing gallery.** When this section was first written 326 were: the static
`apps/landing/src/data/projects.ts` array audit #8 flagged is machine-generated by
`scripts/dev/generate-landing-projects.mjs`, and it had gone 328 entries against a
501-cartridge commons because the generator silently emits a short list in a checkout
without the submodules. Two of those 328 entries were cartridges the Commons catalog
deliberately does not publish, one of them the client-private `tablaco`. #100 regenerated
the file from a complete checkout (501 entries, `tablaco` dropped on both the signals the
backend uses — `access_control.view == "private"` and `PRIVATE_PROJECTS`), and closed the
hole that let it rot: `--check` blocks a stale commit in `manifest-validation`, the only
lane whose checkout is complete enough to judge it, and `build-landing` **regenerates** at
deploy so the shipped gallery is correct by construction. The one remaining entry the
catalog does not publish is the `cq-hyperobject-test` engine fixture. This was the largest
single value gap the recomputation found, and it is the one that closed.

**Licences.** All 500 cartridges carry an explicit licence: 496 CERN-OHL-W-2.0 and four
single instances of other licences, one of which is NonCommercial. A fifth cartridge
declares CERN-OHL-W-2.0 but carries a documented NonCommercial exposure from vendored
upstream files, which the catalog surfaces as `license_exposure`. Counts only — choosing or
changing a licence is not this document's call.

**Verification blocks.** 9 of 500 cartridges carry a `verification` block, and none of the
176 added since the audit do. The 2026-08 audit did not measure this at all; at 1.8% it is
now the emptiest field in the manifest model that the model actually defines.

**Animations.** 1 of 500. Unchanged in absolute terms since the audit — audit #3b's "S to
mount, M to make useful" reads exactly as it did, against a denominator 53% larger.

### Reproducing this section

```bash
git submodule update --init projects    # the commons; `update = none` mounts are skipped
python3 scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py            # the table above
python3 scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py --cohorts  # audit-era vs added-since
python3 scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py --json     # machine-readable
python3 scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py --write    # refresh the generated table
python3 scripts/qa/value_extraction_audit.py --check    # what CI runs
```

`git submodule update` honours `update = none`, so the two private client cartridges —
which mount at `private-projects/` since RFC 0038 P2, outside the public commons — stay
empty. Do not add `--checkout`, which overrides that and tries to clone them. They are
excluded from every count here, exactly as `COMMONS.md` excludes them.
